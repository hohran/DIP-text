With countless available software services, they often need to behave based on a set of rules.
Such set of rules is called a policy and it could describe rate-limits, permitted network routes, or accounts a user can withdraw money from.
For a service to be successful, it needs to make sure, that its policy is valid and kept.

Usually, policies are hard-coded into the services, which often makes them difficult to maintain, since they are spread into multiple files and contexts.
Since they play a crucial part in the safety of today's internet, there has been a great effort to specify and evaluate them separate of the main source code.
This has lead to the creation of policy agents, which are run parallel to the main program and queries are sent to them to evaluate the correctness of actions, according to the specified policy.
This makes the service much easier to maintain, scale, and improves the ability to discover violations and conflicts.

Policy agents reason about rules, which are usually specified in dedicated languages.
In this day and age, the most prevalent ones are: Rego by Open Policy Agent (OPA), Cedar, and OpenFGA.

This work focuses on a analysis of policies specified in the Rego policy language.
To do this, each policy is converted into a \emph{Satisfiability modulo theories} (\emph{SMT}) formula.
The conversion to this state should allow us to reason over these policies more generally and efficiently, using a dedicated \emph{SMT solver}, such as Z3.
For example one could ask, whether rules of one policy are fully contained in another one.
Another relevant case is to test policies symbolically, i.e., not on concrete examples, but on a possibly infinite set of examples at once.
